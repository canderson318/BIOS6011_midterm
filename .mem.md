# Simulating SGS read counts (n x p): notes

## Model
- Counts across p features are **multinomial**, not multivariate binomial: `X_i ~ Mult(N_i, theta_i)`, rows sum to library size `N_i`.
- Sampling noise is ~normal (large N*theta) or ~Poisson (rare features), not lognormal.
- Lognormal belongs to the biological layer (true abundances across features/samples).
- **Dirichlet** generates proportions `theta` on the simplex, not counts. Dirichlet + multinomial = Dirichlet-multinomial (overdispersed counts).

## Generative order
1. `m ~ lognormal(0, m_sigma)`, then `m /= m.sum()` (mean composition)
2. `alpha = m * conc` (conc = sum of alpha)
3. `theta ~ Dirichlet(alpha)` per sample (n x p, rows sum to 1)
4. `N ~ lognormal(log(median), N_sigma)`, clipped to e.g. [1e5, 1e7], cast to int
5. `X ~ Multinomial(N_i, theta_i)` (n x p), `X.sum(1) == N`

## Alpha
- `E[theta_j] = alpha_j / sum(alpha)`: relative alpha sets mean abundance.
- `sum(alpha)` = concentration: high means samples cluster tightly around the mean profile; low means spiky, sample-specific, sparse.
- `alpha_j < 1` makes feature j often ~0 and occasionally large; per-feature CV is about `1/sqrt(alpha_j)`.
- Clumping is across *samples*, not between features. Dirichlet gives only mild negative correlation between features.
- Parameterize shape (`m`) and concentration separately. A raw `lognormal` alpha ties sum(alpha) to p and sigma.

## Lognormal sigma
- sigma is the SD on the log scale. `exp(sigma)` is the multiplicative factor around the **median** (not the mean).
- Mean is `exp(mu + sigma^2/2)`, CV is `sqrt(exp(sigma^2) - 1)`.
- Rule: sigma = ln(f)/2 gives 95% of values within /f to *f of the median. State the coverage (68/95/99.7%) when saying "N-fold".
- `ln(1000)` as sigma means 1000x typical deviation, which is far too wide.

## Checks
- `X / X.sum(1)` will not equal `theta` (multinomial noise, sd = sqrt(theta(1-theta)/N)). `np.allclose` at default tolerance fails.
- Better checks: row sums, z-scores `(p_hat - theta)/sd` should have mean 0 and std 1, mean-variance plot above the Poisson line.

## Sparsity
- Zero fraction rises as alpha_j falls. Rare features with a small `alpha_j` are mostly Dirichlet zeros, not sampling zeros.
- Gamma-Poisson estimate: `P(X=0) ~ (1 + N/sum(alpha))^(-alpha_j)`.
- Flat `m` (small m_sigma) with large conc gives few zeros (~8%). Tiny alpha_j gives many (~91%).
- Use m_sigma ~ 1.5-2.5 for realistic abundance spread, then sweep `conc` to hit the target zero fraction (e.g. 70-80%).
- Dirichlet can't give rare features that are both often present and highly variable. If needed, use logistic-normal (`z ~ MVN(mu, Sigma)`, softmax, multinomial), zero-inflation, or SparseDOSSA2.
